## D0. Erratum — the original pixel-diff formula was measuring the wrong thing

**What was wrong:** the first pass's pixel-diff used `PIL.ImageChops.difference` on the raw RGBA export and counted *any* pixel where *any* channel differed by even 1 out of 255 ("naive diff"). This formula assumed the exported PNGs were transparent (alpha varying 0–255 at the glyph edges), so that "any-channel-differs" would only fire on real ink. **That assumption was false.** Direct pixel inspection shows Figma's screenshot export flattens every icon onto an **opaque white background** — alpha is 255 everywhere, always, and the only signal is the grayscale ink value (e.g. `(77,77,77,255)` for the glyph, `(255,255,255,255)` for background). Because two independently-drawn vector paths for "the same" icon are almost never anchored at byte-identical sub-pixel coordinates, their anti-aliased edge ramps (the 1–2px band of partial-gray pixels where ink fades to white) land on slightly different pixels — and the naive "any channel differs by ≥1" test flags nearly the entire edge-ramp band as "different," even when the actual ink shape is visually identical. On a 24×24 icon, that edge-ramp band is a large fraction of total pixels, which is why the original numbers were inflated across the board (most severely on icons with thin strokes or many small curves).

**How this was caught:** an independent re-render/diff reported 0.8% (Warning) and 1.1% (StarOutline) against this report's original 14.6% / 25.3%, with direct visual inspection calling them indistinguishable. Re-rendering both pairs fresh (new `get_screenshot` calls, not reused files) reproduced the exact original 14.6%/25.3% — ruling out a one-off rendering glitch — which meant the discrepancy had to be in the *formula*, not the images. Inspecting raw pixel tuples confirmed alpha is always 255 (no transparency to speak of) and that the differing pixels are concentrated in a thin band precisely where an edge-antialiasing offset would put them.

**Corrected formula:** convert both renders to RGB, classify each pixel as **"ink"** if its (R+G+B)/3 luminance is below **200** (i.e., visibly darker than the white background) or **"background"** otherwise, then count the fraction of the 576 pixels where that binary ink/background classification disagrees between Filled and Outlined. This measures actual silhouette disagreement instead of sub-pixel edge-ramp noise. A secondary continuous metric, **mean absolute RGB difference** (average `|Δchannel|` across all pixels/channels, as a % of the 0–255 scale), is reported alongside it for pairs where a magnitude reading is more useful than a binary in/out call. **No threshold beyond this ink/background split (luminance < 200) is applied** — every pixel where the two images disagree on ink-vs-background counts equally, regardless of how far past 200 the luminance sits.

**All 94 pairs were recomputed with the corrected formula** (Section D below fully replaces the original table; the original per-pair numbers should be discarded). The reconciliation with the independently-reported 0.8%/1.1% is *directional and large* — Warning drops from 14.6% → 6.6%, StarOutline from 25.3% → 3.6% — but does not land on exactly 0.8%/1.1%; the residual gap is most likely a further difference in edge-tolerance choice between the two methods (e.g. a small dilation/erosion tolerance around the silhouette boundary, which this method does not apply), not a remaining bug — the diff-mask visualizations below show the residual disagreement is a thin, plausible anti-aliasing-adjacent band, not a gross shape difference, for both of these two pairs specifically. Cloud, by contrast, barely moved (32.1% → 28.8%) because its disagreement is a solid interior block, not an edge band — see the diff-mask below.
